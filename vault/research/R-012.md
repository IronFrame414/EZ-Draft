## 0. Code editions

- 8th Edition (2023) FBC took effect on December 31, 2023 ([FBC Effective Dates](https://www.floridabuilding.org/fbc/Publications/2023_Effective_Dates.pdf), read).
- 9th Edition (2026) FBC is scheduled to take effect on 12/31/2026, six months after publication. The final rule hearing is still listed as "TBD" in the August 2026 workplan, so the date is planned, not final ([2026 FBC Workplan, Aug 2026](https://www.floridabuilding.org/fbc/commission/FBC_0726/Commission/2026_FBC_Workplan_August_2026.pdf), read). The catalog already carries `section_now` and `section_next` for every rule.
- One- and two-family plumbing and mechanical work falls under FBC-Residential (R101.2 scope: one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses up to three stories). Plumbing is Chapters 25–33 and mechanical is Chapters 12–23 ([FBC-R 2023 Ch. 1](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-residential-code-2023/chapter/1/scope-and-administration); table of contents on [FBC-R 2023 Ch. 15](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-residential-code-2023/chapter/15/exhaust-systems)). Every catalog plumbing and mechanical rule already cites FBC-R 2023 (`code` = FBC-R, URL FLRC2023P1).

## 1. Plumbing drawings and calculations

**What the code requires**
- FBC-Building 107.3.5 sets the minimum plan review criteria. Its Plumbing list (fixture requirements, water supply piping, sanitary drainage, water heaters, vents, plumbing riser and more) sits under **Commercial Buildings**. The **Residential (one- and two-family)** list has no plumbing or mechanical items: it covers site, fire, egress, structure, flood and accessible bath. Exceptions: no plan review for replacing existing equipment such as mechanical units and water heaters, or for minor electrical, plumbing and mechanical repairs ([FBC-B 2023 Ch. 1, 107.3.5](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-building-code-2023/chapter/1/scope-and-administration), read).
- 107.2.1: construction documents must show the location, nature and extent of the work in enough detail to show it complies ([same page](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-building-code-2023/chapter/1/scope-and-administration)).
- No section found in FBC-R Ch. 25 (P2501) or FBC-B 107 asks for an isometric diagram or a fixture-unit table on residential plans. An engineered plumbing design does need floor plans and a riser diagram (snippet only, section not confirmed; `retriever-web-fbc-permit-plumbing-hvac-scope.md`).

**What departments ask for**
- Unincorporated PBC, Type 3(A) checklist (1 & 2 unit dwelling addition and interior/exterior alteration), item 37: "Plumbing riser, as applies". Item 36: gas plans per 106.3.5.2, as applies. The footer is dated 10/6/05 and cites the FBC 2004 amendments, so it is old but still posted ([Type 3a Application Sufficiency Checklist](https://discover.pbc.gov/pzb/building/Forms/Type%203a%20Application%20Sufficiency%20Checklist.pdf), read).
- PBC Type 2(A) (incidental, demolition and replacement work), item 29: "Riser diagrams" ([Type 2a Checklist](https://discover.pbc.gov/pzb/building/Forms/Type%202a%20Application%20Sufficiency%20Checklist.pdf), read).
- PBC like-for-like water heater replacement is self-issued on a form with no drawing ([Water Heater Change-out](https://discover.pbc.gov/pzb/building/PDF/Water%20Heater%20Change-out.pdf), per the retriever).
- Boca Raton's Plumbing Piping/Fixtures checklist asks only for "Contract" and "Plans", with no drawing detail ([Plumbing Piping Fixtures](https://www.myboca.us/DocumentCenter/View/269/Plumbing-Piping-Fixtures-PDF-), read).
- West Palm Beach: plumbing needs a permit regardless of cost. No plumbing drawing checklist was found ([Permits FAQ](https://www.wpb.org/Departments/Development-Services/Building-Division/Permits-FAQ)).
- Wellington's Plumbing checklist (FBC 8th Ed.) asks for "Construction documents/plans" and a scope of work with the total replacement value per FBC 109.3. Plans from a design professional must be signed, sealed and digitally verifiable ([Plumbing checklist](https://www.wellingtonfl.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4943/Plumbing_FINAL-es), read, first page).
- **No department page reviewed asks for DFU or WSFU tables or pipe-sizing calculations for a residential remodel.** What departments name is a plumbing riser diagram.

## 2. Mechanical drawings and calculations

**What the code requires**
- Sizing: FBC-EC R403.7.1 requires Manual S equipment sizing from Manual J loads and the indoor and outdoor model numbers with capacities ([FBC-EC 2023 Ch. 4](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-energy-conservation-code-2023/chapter/RE_4/re-residential-energy-efficiency); catalog EN-037–EN-039). FBC-R M1401.3 and M1601.1 cover Manual S/J and Manual D (catalog M-023, M-072).
- **Alterations**: FBC-EC R503.1.2 says new heating, cooling and duct systems that are part of an alteration must meet R403.1, R403.2, R403.3 and R403.6. It does **not** list R403.7 (sizing). Duct extensions with less than 40 linear ft in unconditioned space are not leak-tested ([FBC-EC 2023 Ch. 5](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-energy-conservation-code-2023/chapter/RE_5/re-existing-buildings), read).
- **Equipment change-out**: R501.7 requires new or replaced HVAC to meet minimum efficiency. Outdoor and indoor units must be a matched system, verified by AHRI data, an accredited lab, a manufacturer's letter or a Florida PE letter. Reused ducts need not meet code if meeting it would mean replacing them. R501.7.1: existing equipment need not meet current efficiency, "including system sizing and duct sealing" ([same page](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-energy-conservation-code-2023/chapter/RE_5/re-existing-buildings)).
- Space newly conditioned (R503.2): must fully comply ([same page](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-energy-conservation-code-2023/chapter/RE_5/re-existing-buildings)).
- FBC-B 107.3.5 exception: replacing mechanical units needs no plan review ([FBC-B Ch. 1](https://up.codes/viewer/florida/fl-building-code-2023/chapter/1/scope-and-administration)).

## 4. Code rule catalog: covered vs missing

The catalog has 174 plumbing rules (P-001–P-174: 122 Reminder, 34 Needs data, 18 Automatic) and 108 mechanical rules (M-001–M-108), plus energy rules EN-018–EN-062 (vault/spec/code-rules.json).

**Covered**: DFU counts (P-126, Automatic); branch and stack limits (P-140); building drain (P-141); trap sizes (P-174); shower drain (P-039); vent and wet vent size (P-157, P-163, Reminder); pressure, PRV and manifold lines (P-100, P-105, P-109); dryer duct length (M-053); bath fan duct (M-066); exhaust rates (M-071); kitchen hood (M-061); Manual S/J/D (M-023, M-072, EN-037); capacity limits (EN-038, EN-039); alteration and change-out energy rules (EN-024, EN-043–EN-062). EN-059's check text matches R503.1.2 above.

**Missing or weak**
1. **Water supply sizing has no numbers.** P-108 is a Reminder. Its WSFU values are only in its `note` field, and the Table P2903.6 demand and Table P2903.7 pipe-size values are not in any check text. EZ Draft cannot compute supply pipe sizes until the catalog has them (calc-auditor).
2. **No drawing-content rules.** No rule says a plumbing riser or isometric diagram, an equipment schedule or a duct layout must be on the set. These are department requirements (section 1, section 2), not code rules, so they belong in the submittal profile or a sheet checklist, not in code-rules.json.
3. **No scope-match rule (Q-0180).** Nothing links a plumbing or mechanical scope line to a sheet.
4. **Duplicate**: P-080 and M-106 both cover M2005.2 (water heater locations), with slightly different wording.
5. M-066 has only six cells of Table M1506.2 in its check text; other fan, diameter and duct-type combinations cannot be computed (calc-auditor).
6. EN-039 ("within 4 kW") does not say whether a heat strip below the load is allowed, and changing Btu/h to kW needs a cited factor (calc-auditor).

## 5. Worked examples (calc-auditor, numbers from catalog check text only)

The calc-auditor used catalog check text only, as CLAUDE.md rule 13 requires, and did not read the code text. Values are as the catalog states them, and every rule is still "not yet verified" until EZD-1710 (vault/KNOWN-LIMITS.md).

**A. Drainage fixture units (P-126, P-140, P-141).** Add the d.f.u. of new fixtures (use the group value when a whole group is added) to the existing load, then compare with the pipe-size limit.
1. Hall bath, full group: 5 on a 3 in. branch (limit 20): pass.
2. Primary bath: 2 lavatories + shower + tub + water closet = 1+1+2+2+3 = 9 on a 2 in. branch (limit 6): fail. The water closet is also on a pipe under 3 in.: fail. On a 3 in. branch, 9 ≤ 20: pass.
3. Whole-house re-plumb: 6 full baths 30 + half bath 4 + kitchen 2 + laundry 3 = 39. A 3 in. building drain at 1/8 in./ft (limit 36): fail. At 1/4 in./ft (limit 42): pass. 4 in. at 1/8 in./ft (180): pass.

**B. Water supply fixture units (P-108 note only).** Totals only, because the table values are missing (section 4, item 1).
1. Full bath 3.6. 2. Kitchen group 2.5. 3. Three full baths 10.8 + half 2.6 + kitchen 2.5 + laundry 2.5 + two hose bibbs 5.0 = 23.4.

Checks that can be computed today: static pressure 85 psi is over 80, so a PRV is needed (P-105). Manifold line 3/8 in. at 45 ft and 55 psi is within 60 ft and at least 40 psi: pass (P-109).

**C. Dryer duct (M-053).** Straight length plus fitting allowances must be 35 ft or less; the transition duct is not counted.
1. 6 ft + one 6 in. radius 90° (1.75) = 7'-9": pass.
2. 22 ft + two mitered 90° (2×5) + one mitered 45° (2.5) = 34'-6": pass.
3. 24 ft + three mitered 90° = 39 ft: fail. With 10 in. radius 90s, 24 + 4.5 = 28'-6": pass.

**D. Bath fan duct (M-066).** Allowed length = table value − 15 ft per elbow.
1. 50 cfm, 4 in. flex, 12 ft, 2 elbows: 56 − 30 = 26 ≥ 12: pass.
2. 80 cfm, 4 in. flex, 8 ft, 1 elbow: 4 − 15 < 0: fail. The same run in 4 in. smooth: 31 − 15 = 16 ≥ 8: pass.
3. 100 cfm, 4 in. smooth, 6 ft, 1 elbow: 10 − 15 < 0: fail. In 5 in. smooth: 91 − 15 = 76: pass.

**E. Local exhaust rates (M-071, M-062).** Bath at least 50 cfm intermittent or 20 continuous, rated at 0.25 in. w.c. Kitchen at least 100 intermittent or 25 continuous.
1. Bath fan 50 cfm at 0.25 in.: pass.
2. Fan sold as "80 cfm" but rated 45 cfm at 0.25 in.: fail.
3. Kitchen hood 300 cfm: pass, with no makeup air at 400 or less. A 600 cfm hood needs makeup air unless the Florida single-family exception applies (needs data).
The 9th Edition lets the rate be met at one or more fan speeds (catalog M-071).

**F. HVAC capacity vs Manual J (EN-037–EN-039).** Manual J is an input; EZ Draft does not compute it. Total capacity must be at least the load and at most 1.15 × the load, and latent capacity at least the latent load.
1. Load 30,000 Btu/h, capacity 33,600 (limit 34,500), latent 7,000 ≥ 6,000: pass.
2. Load 24,000, capacity 34,800 (limit 27,600): fail, unless no closer size exists.
3. Heat pump: load 42,000, capacity 46,200 ≤ 48,300: pass, but latent 8,400 < 9,000: fail.
Resistance heat within 4 kW: 10 kW on a 6 kW load: pass; 15 kW: fail.

## 6. Round 3 questions for Josh (recommendation first)

1. **Plumbing sheet content.** Recommend: one plumbing sheet per permit set with fixture locations (new, moved, existing) on the proposed plan plus a riser diagram generated from the same fixtures, labeled with pipe sizes. This is what PBC (Type 3(A) item 37) and the code's plumbing list name. Isometric view: optional. Do you agree, or do your departments ever ask for more?
2. **Plumbing calculations.** Recommend: show a DFU table (example A) on the riser sheet for every job that adds fixtures, and a WSFU and supply-size table only on whole-house re-plumbs. No department page asks for either, so they are for your own check and the plan reviewer's comfort. Do you agree?
3. **Supply pipe sizing data.** Recommend: have the calc-auditor add the Table P2903.6 and P2903.7 values to the catalog before supply sizing is built (section 4, item 1). OK?
4. **HVAC: when to require Manual J.** Recommend: require a Manual J load input (you or your HVAC sub supply the report; EZ Draft runs only the capacity check F) when new equipment is added or the system changes size in an alteration, or space becomes conditioned. For a like-for-like change-out, produce the equipment data form and AHRI matched-system proof only, with no drawings. Boca asks for Manual J only when the old unit's data is missing. Who does your Manual J today?
5. **Mechanical sheet.** Recommend: an equipment schedule (model numbers, sensible and total capacity) plus a plan with the air handler, condenser, supply and return locations and duct runs, for alterations only (PBC Type 3(A) items 32–33). Include exhaust fans and dryer vent on this sheet with checks C, D and E. Agree?
6. **Change-out forms.** PBC, Boca, Wellington and North Palm Beach each have their own AC change-out form. Should EZ Draft fill these forms, or only print an equipment data block you copy from?
7. **Scope match (Q-0180).** Recommend: every scope line tagged plumbing, mechanical or electrical must have at least one sheet of that trade, and every trade sheet must have a scope line; a mismatch blocks export until fixed or overridden with a reason. Block or warn?
8. **Old PBC checklists.** The only published PBC residential checklists are from 2005. Do you have a current PBC reviewer comment list or rejection letters we can use instead?
