| EZD-1972 | Where the first open of 3D spent its time (measured 2026-11-24, build Mac, headless Chromium on its software renderer, the renderer CI uses) | Not in building meshes: the scene of the benchmark project is built in about 35 ms and a later frame of it is drawn in about 30 ms. About 110 to 190 ms of each first open was the graphics driver building two shaders the first time the 3D scene was drawn: the lit surface shader, and a flat-shaded copy of it asked for only by the scene's frame-counting mesh (it had no normals). Drawing each kind as one merged mesh (4 draws for 1,225), one side only, or without antialiasing made no measurable difference, so none of those was changed | AC-EZD-1972-1; plan EZD-1972 "shader compilation" |
| EZD-1972 | How it is made faster | Two changes. (1) The plan's first frame also draws one triangle too small to cover a pixel with the 3D scene's material and lights, so the driver builds that shader while the plan loads (the plan's option "prebuild on idle after the plan loads"); the lights and triangle leave after that frame. No 3D scene code is loaded and no mesh is built before the first press of 3D (11 rule 7, AC-EZD-1001a-2 unchanged). (2) The frame-counting mesh has the attributes of every other mesh, so it needs no second shader. The material and lights are made in one place (app/src/editor/plan/scene-warmup.ts) for the warm-up and the scene, and a unit test fails if a scene mesh or light would need another shader. Either change alone left the time unchanged (182 to 228 ms with the warm-up alone) | AC-EZD-1972-1; AC-EZD-1972-3 |
